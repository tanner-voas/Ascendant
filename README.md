# Ascendant — Tactical Battleground (v0.1.8)

Initial, classless 5v5 prototype for a future LitRPG cultivation role-playing game, loosely inspired by Pathfinder. No classes, magic, feats, gear, levels, or unique abilities yet. This is a standalone app and a lazy-loaded AppLauncher module; no external dependencies or backend.

## Run

```sh
python3 package.py
npm test
python3 -m http.server 8765 --directory dist/standalone
```

Open `http://localhost:8765`. Standalone PWA assets are in `dist/standalone`; AppLauncher consumes `dist/ascendant`. Both are generated, not committed. Cloudflare Workers assets config is included. `main` is the initial branch; `origin` is the intended `https://github.com/tanner-voas/Ascendant.git` (create the empty remote and push before deploying the launcher).

## Play

- Design and name five characters per side. Each must have 200 integer points, 10–40 per attribute. Roll 10d4 independently for each attribute and normalize by proportional allocation with bounded integer correction. Draft and complete teams are saved locally. Lock individual attributes to preserve them when rolling a character, balancing, or rolling a team. Impossible locked allocations leave the character/team unchanged. The derived-stat panel updates as you edit. Save named characters and teams to the local library, copy them between slots/sides, rename/duplicate/delete saved entries, or import/export validated JSON rosters.
- Select one of 20 fixed seeded procedural arenas, optionally enter a battle seed. Positions are randomized in disjoint team zones. Both dice and initiative use the battle seed.
- Turn order is continuous simulated time, not a fixed round. Control the currently active Team A member. Click/tap enemies or their roster cards to target; click/tap empty ground to add a straight segment, or drag to its endpoint. Undo, clear, and commit the path. Pan with a drag in Pan mode, pinch to zoom, or use zoom/fit/center-active controls. Distinct gold, white and dashed orange rings mark the active, inspected and targeted units. A path is rejected if it crosses a solid obstacle, another living character, the map edge, or its movement budget. Movement once per turn, then one action; no movement is required.
- Fixed actions: strike, heavy strike, quick strike, shove (off-balance/displacement), guard, recover, observe, wait. Strikes use opposed d20 rolls and 2d6 variation, with range and line-of-sight. Zero HP incapacitates. No low-health or low-mana performance penalties.
- AI uses the same movement/action API; prioritizes weak/nearby targets and allies' engagements, navigates around obstacles with a visibility graph, and selects strikes, guard or recovery. Auto-play both sides, pause/resume, step, choose AI speed, inspect timeline/resources/stats and export JSON log. Action previews display exact opposed-d20 hit probabilities, expected pre-overkill damage including misses, damage range on successful hits, resource expenditures and next-action delay from the same calculation functions as resolution. Expand log entries for dice, mitigation, costs and timing. A 1,000-turn ceiling produces a draw. Battles autosave after actions, including movement, PRNG state and UI state; Save & setup returns to the roster editor. Resume restores the saved battle with AI paused. Restart same seed uses the original rosters and map, not any subsequently edited setup. New random seed reuses those rosters/map. Starting a different battle replaces the single saved battle slot after confirmation.

## Mechanics / provisional calibration

`src/engine.js` contains the independent deterministic engine. `src/maps.js` creates 20 arenas; `src/app.js` renders the UI. Standard stats are 20; HP and the four other capacities follow the previously proposed power-law formulas. Physical, mental and resolve efficiency falls quadratically, with nonzero floors; resolve can offset exhaustion at a cost. Mana is present but unused by intentionally mundane actions. Health recovery is not available mid-fight in this prototype; recover replenishes energy/resolve. Innate DR is flat and RES proportional, with penetration function support. Heavy strikes have provisional flat penetration. Turn cost uses the approved cosine penalty with Agility scaling, resets next turn, and clusters closely spaced bends by travelled distance (not a full curvature/physics model). Movement adds a modest time and energy cost. Off-balance is the initial applied graded status; fear is represented in engine calculations but no current action inflicts it. The complete proposed damage-type/condition catalogue, injuries, death rules, spells, progression and future WorldGen/Chronicle integration are not implemented. These numerical combat values are sandbox defaults, not final balance rules.

`npm test` checks 10,000 stat rolls, deterministic deployment across all maps, path collision/turning, damage penetration, zero-resource action availability, and full AI matches on all 20 maps. New tests cover 10,000 locked allocations, roster JSON validation, exact restoration of the PRNG and replayed future events on 20 arenas, and exhaustive dice combinations for four attack actions. No overlap or out-of-bounds positions allowed for living units.

## v0.1.2 — Movement and positioning

- **Path editor:** tap or drag on ground to append a segment; tap a numbered waypoint to select it, drag it to relocate it, tap a midpoint `+` to insert a point, or delete the selected waypoint. Undo removes the last point; Clear discards the path. Tap Auto path and then a destination to generate a route; Route to target plans to a legal melee position. Routes stop at the current turn's budget. Trim path clips the existing plan to its longest legal prefix. Neither preview nor route generation consumes dice. The final path is always validated before movement commits.
- **Map overlays:** blue sampled direct-reach envelope (from the current path endpoint if planning a path); red enemy engagement circles, faded after interception has been spent; orange interception points along the planned path; white facing indicators. The reach envelope is an approximation for one additional straight segment, not the complete set of destinations reachable by detours; individual segments are validated exactly. The threat circle shows nominal range, while actual interception requires line of sight. Pan/pinch/zoom remain available. Face mode changes facing on the current character's turn, without consuming movement; the final movement segment updates facing, and a melee attack faces its target.
- **Terrain:** four passable difficult-terrain rectangles per original seeded arena, with brush ×1.25, loose stone ×1.40 and soft ground ×1.18. Obstacles and deployment geometry are preserved. Exact covered segment lengths determine terrain cost; overlapping terrain, if present in a custom map, uses the highest applicable multiplier. Separate UI fields report actual distance, turn cost and terrain cost, including after commitment.

Provisional movement mode multipliers (relative to Run; the budget is further adjusted by fatigue, conditions, Agility and Resolve use):

| Mode | Budget | Energy per budget metre | Momentum coefficient | Movement delay contribution | Interception attack modifier |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Walk | .72 | .50 | .72 | .85 | -1 |
| Run | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 |
| Sprint | 1.35 | 1.65 | 1.28 | 1.12 | +2 |
| Cautious | .58 | .72 | .60 | 1.12 | -4 |

Momentum cost coefficient is `0.15 * normalMovement * (20/AGI)^0.25 * mode.turn`. An isolated bend costs `k*(1-cos(angle))`; for a short approach the coefficient is multiplied by `0.75 + 0.25*min(1,approachMetres/2)`. Consecutive bends separated by at most one metre belong to a cluster. The cluster charges the greater of the individual cosine sum or the cosine cost of its total absolute angle (two units of cost for each full 180° plus the remainder). This prevents tightly subdivided turns from avoiding cost while allowing large, sweeping arcs. There is no initial or terminal turn penalty and no carry-over between turns. Collinear segmentation does not affect costs.

Engagement is a 1.9 m visible reach. Leaving a living opponent's engagement may trigger a single opposed-d20 interception until that defender's next turn; entering alone does not. Interception consumes defender energy, can deal reduced strike damage, and immediately stops the move if the mover reaches zero Health. Movement mode affects exposure. Side attacks receive +1, rear attacks +2, with another +1 for an ally engaging from the opposite direction (>120°). These are provisional positional modifiers, not new skills. All fixed actions remain available; no spells/classes or equipment have been introduced. AI uses the same path validation, movement mode costs and combat rules; routing includes difficult terrain and approximate incoming heading costs, and does not promise a globally optimal route.

Old v0.1.1 saves resume with default facing and available interceptions. The saved positions, resources and PRNG state are preserved, but future battle results may differ because the rules changed. Same-version resume and rematch remain deterministic. The standalone cache includes navigation.js automatically; no launcher source change is required. Update the launcher submodule pointer after pushing this release.

Validation: `npm test` includes previous allocation, dice-distribution and determinism checks plus momentum-subdivision, terrain, mode, facing, interception, fatal interruption, pathfinding, old-save migration and randomized arena tests. The randomized audit completes 100 full 5v5 matches (five per arena) with no draws or invalid positions. Browser navigation is restricted in the release environment; visual/touch testing on actual iOS remains outstanding.

## v0.1.3 — Time and action economy

Combat now uses an event-driven, continuous simulated clock; there is no real-time pressure on the player. A decision starts a serializable job, and the scheduler resolves preparation, execution/impact, and recovery events in chronological order. Other units can choose and complete actions while the job is pending. At equal timestamps pending events precede new decisions, then stable unit IDs break ties. A strong hit can interrupt an unfinished ordinary action; a heavy strike is committed once execution starts, though forced displacement still interrupts it. Costs are paid on commitment, including on cancellation or target loss. The actual target range, line of sight, facing, target defense and available reaction are checked at impact. Forecasts are conditional on the current battlefield, not a guarantee. Attacker exertion efficiency is locked on commitment.

Timing coefficients live in `src/timeline.js`. At 20 in every attribute and full energy, a strike takes 0.528s preparation, 1.152s execution, and 0.720s recovery (2.4s total). Reaction scales preparation and recovery separately from execution, which depends on Agility/Strength for physical actions and Intelligence for analysis/holding. Physical, mental and resolve efficiency still matter. Movement has its own phases and keeps the same v0.1.2 movement allowance and path costs. It progresses through waypoints and intermediate checkpoints (at most 1.25m apart); collision and interception are checked again at each checkpoint. The unit gets a follow-up action after movement recovery, even if the route was interrupted; no second move or additional reaction refresh is granted. Movement is not continuous collision physics, and full planned energy is charged up front.

The default defensive policy is Dodge; choose Dodge, Brace, Counter, or None at the decision prompt. Dodge modifies the opposed attack, Brace improves mitigation, and Counter permits a reduced damage retaliation on a miss. A shared reaction allowance also governs existing disengagement interceptions. None disables the new strike responses, not the existing disengagement interception. Rear attacks and committed attacks can deny reactions. Enemy AI chooses a reaction based on its stats and current energy. Wait delays the next decision; Hold for enemy wakes once an opponent enters engagement range (or times out), without automatically attacking.

The timeline panel shows all pending phases and anticipated completion times, with dashed paths/telegraphs on the board. Toggle event-by-event resolution, pause AI, and step pending events manually. The latest 180 event/decision frames are retained for read-only playback; this is not an undo system or a full battle recording. Pending jobs, reaction states, PRNG, follow-up state, and recent frames persist in saved battles. Old v0.1.1/v0.1.2 saves migrate to the new scheduler; continuing them can produce different results from their old rules. Existing roster format and app ID are unchanged.

No classes, spells, feats, equipment, advancement, or new player attributes have been added. Timing/reaction balance is provisional.

## v0.1.4 — Damage and defenses

- `src/damage.js` is the common typed damage engine. Its 13 supported types are blunt, slashing, piercing, heat, cold, electrical, corrosive, sonic, toxic, arcane, mental, vital and spatial. Only blunt damage is currently delivered by the fixed unarmed attacks; there are still no spells, classes or equipment.
- Innate DR = `2*(B/20)^0.8`; innate RES = `0.6*(B/20)^0.75/(4+(B/20)^0.75)`. B is END, except for mental damage where it is `(END+WIL)/2`. Defensive sources add DR and multiply remaining damage fractions. Guard and bodily reactions apply only to physical impact types.
- `resolveDamage(components, profile)` resolves a single simultaneous impact. Each component has a type, raw damage, and optional penetration object (`drFlat`, `drRatio`, `resFlat`, `resRatio`; RES fractions are 0–1). Proportional penetration applies first, followed by flat penetration. Allocate effective DR by the raw damage fraction of that component, subtract it, then apply RES. Each separate impact receives its own DR budget. Zero damage is permitted. The result contains per-component defenses, effective penetration, reduction, resistance and final damage.
- Strike, quick strike, heavy strike, interceptions and counters use the typed engine. Heavy strike ignores 1 DR plus 8% proportional DR and 10% proportional RES (proportional applied before flat). Coefficients are provisional, pending the balance release.
- Opposed d20 degrees: margin >=12 critical success (1.4x raw damage on damaging attacks), >=0 ordinary success, <=-12 critical failure (ordinary attack attempts cause 5 off-balance on the attacker), otherwise failure. No automatic hit/miss based on natural rolls. Existing strong-hit interruption and forced-displacement rules remain.
- Block trades more physical energy for reduced incoming impact (STR/END); parry trades mental energy for a precision-based defensive contest (REA/AGI/PER). Dodge, brace and counter remain available. All share the same reaction allowance and facing restrictions.
- Forecasts enumerate opposed d20s and 2d6 using the actual damage engine without consuming random state. They display hit and critical probabilities, expected raw / DR / RES / final damage, conditional damage range and relevant penetration. The inspector exposes all 13 defenses; combat logs expose the exact precision contributions and typed damage breakdown. These are current-state forecasts, not predictions of changes before impact.
- Existing v1 save schema is retained; derived defenses are recalculated during restore, and existing jobs resolve under the updated rules. No launcher source changes required.

## v0.1.5 — Resources and exertion

- `src/resources.js` contains the resource model. Health and Mana are absolute; no fatigue penalty depends on them. In combat Health does not regenerate; Mana passively recovers but remains unused. Physical, Mental, and Resolve have quadratic efficiency curves with floors 42%, 45%, and 40%. Expenditure clamps to actual reserves, not a new zero-resource action prohibition (including reactions). Costs and committed output are fixed at scheduling; no refund on interruption.
- Normal effort is 100%; the player can select 50–100% in 10% increments. Output multiplier is `0.40 + 0.60 * effort`; expenditure multiplier is `0.25 + 0.75 * effort^2`. Attack damage, precision, shove force, guarding, observation and movement benefit from higher effort. Recover, Wait and Hold have fixed full effort. Reaction timing remains independent of this optional intensity; movement timing incorporates intensity. The four movement modes and segmented-path geometry are unchanged.
- Physical and mental exhaustion affect different action channels; e.g. heavy strike weights 90/5/5, observation 0/95/5, movement 95/0/5 (P/M/R). Resolve support can be none, physical, mental or both, restoring at most 75% of lost efficiency multiplied by current Resolve efficiency. Cost is weighted by action demand, intensity and fear, capped by remaining Resolve; both boosts scale down together if unaffordable. Movement reserves support for its whole movement allowance, even on short routes, so an inflated budget cannot grant a free boost. Resolve support does not replenish any energy directly.
- Passive recovery per simulated second at 20 stats and no demand is 2 P, 1.8 M, 0.28 R, 0.4 Mana. Stamina/Intelligence and Vitality influence physical/mental rates; Willpower influences Resolve; Spirit influences Mana. Preparation, execution and recovery have different demand multipliers. Resolve recovery slows while exerting it. Recover restores 15 P, 12 M, 5 R at baseline, in addition to passive regeneration. The action preview shows actual resource deductions and a conditional forecast after all phases, excluding enemy interference. Recovery forecasts are estimates, not promises about future battlefield events.
- The AI considers fatigue and vulnerable enemies, varies effort and chooses the relevant Resolve channel, and occasionally recovers in melee when supported by allies. Resource rules are sandbox defaults; comprehensive numerical balance remains a future release. Existing snapshots with boolean Resolve support are accepted. No new class content, magic, or injury effects.

## v0.1.6 — Conditions and status effects

- `src/conditions.js` defines 41 condition prototypes across physical, mobility, sensory, cognitive, volitional, arcane and exhaustion families. Conditions retain source, severity, duration and recovery independently. Graded conditions are continuous 0–100; thresholds can disable movement or skip action opportunities; discrete states are on/off. Grapples are reciprocal relations, not duplicate independent statuses. Low resources are still handled by the resource engine, not by a redundant exhaustion condition.
- Default stacking is diminishing accumulation (`100 * (1 - product(1 - severity/100))`); strongest-only, capped accumulation and independent instances are also supported. Fixed, linear, exponential and explicitly cleared effects are supported. All durations use elapsed simulated seconds rather than turns. Condition damage-over-time uses typed resistance, not repeated per-event flat DR (this models ongoing damage after application); continuous resource drains have separate effects. The current mundane actions do not deliver burning, bleeding, poison or arcane conditions; those are hooks for later content, not new playable abilities.
- Condition resistance is a weighted attribute blend per family. Intimidation is a WIL/STR vs WIL/INT opposed d20 contest at up to 6 metres (requires line of sight and hearing); on success, base fear 42 is adjusted by application power, resistance, another d20 and critical outcome. Fear reduces efficiency and increases costs, never automatically removes control. Trip is an AGI/STR vs mobility resistance contest at 1.7 m: success knocks the target prone until they choose Stand, adds off-balance and interrupts pending actions. Grapple at 1.7 m uses STR/AGI against mobility resistance; both characters lose movement while held, but can perform other legal actions. Escape contests the holder; Release is unconditional. Forced separation, a successful shove, incapacitation or loss of line of sight breaks a grapple. A character cannot hold several targets or chain grapples. AI can use control actions and responds to being tripped/grappled.
- Impairment channels affect relevant resource efficiency, movement, turning, precision, response timing, expenditure and recovery. Same-condition sources combine before channel penalties; independent conditions' channel multipliers are bounded at 25% (sensory loss can reach zero). Explicit incapacitation and immobilization override passive efficiency floors. Legacy off/fear fields from old games remain supported. Positive-HP unconsciousness is temporary and misses decisions until expiry; severe stun skips opportunities while severity is at least 85. At zero HP a character is down and cannot act or recover in combat; ordinary attacks do not establish permanent death, and revival/injury mechanics are deferred.
- Control forecasts enumerate all opposed dice pairs and show estimated fear severity on successful application; effects revalidate at impact. Roster indicators, token markers, condition inspector, severity bars, source durations and battle log details expose active effects. Existing v1 snapshots migrate missing condition arrays and relation fields, with input validation and deterministic RNG restoration. No new classes, skills, level mechanics, equipment or spells were added. All numerical values are provisional sandbox defaults.

## v0.1.7 — Advanced battlefields

- `src/maps.js` exposes 20 deterministic procedural arena profiles, each with independently chosen map variation seed, adjustable dimensions (26–64 × 20–48 m), and symmetric deployment depth (4–8 m, constrained by field width). Battle RNG remains independent of the map seed. Obstacle material, footprint, density, terrain style and relief differ by profile; a broad winding lane and separated deployment areas remain clear. Maps are still intentionally simplified geometric tactical arenas rather than architectural simulations.
- The versioned `ascendant-battlefield-v1` geometry format includes obstacles with physical height and cover, terrain rectangles (traversal factor, stability and concealment), smooth rectangular raised terrain with ramps, deployment zones and environmental hazard regions. It is renderer-independent and an initial candidate for future WorldGen/Chronicle adapters. No integration with those apps yet. Elevation adds a traversal surcharge, uses the highest overlapping terrain factor, modifies attack precision and affects line of sight. Low barricades block movement but allow sight over them; nearby low cover, unstable footing and concealment affect attack accuracy. This model uses ramps, not vertical climbing, falling or multi-storey buildings.
- Hazard-enabled quarry, ravine, canal and ash maps can inflict continuous typed damage, Physical Energy drain and a graded condition (cold, unstable ground or hot ash). Exposure is evaluated over elapsed simulated time at movement checkpoints; the same source refreshes rather than stacking at every scheduler event, and briefly lingers after departure. Damage over time uses Resistance rather than repeatedly charging flat DR. The path preview warns about hazard zones and the pathfinder adds a hazard avoidance cost. Forecasts do not guarantee future exposure or enemy interference.
- Fog of war defaults on for new battlefields and can be disabled in setup. The map geometry is public, while enemy positions and actions outside allied visibility are withheld. Sight is based on individual Perception range, sensory conditions, elevation, sight-blocking geometry and intervening concealment. Close-range hearing assists detection where line of sight is clear. Allied characters share detection, but targeting requires the acting character to detect the target. Darkened areas visualize approximate current team or inspected-ally visibility; question-mark tokens indicate recent last-known enemy locations, not live coordinates. Hidden enemies are not included in planned collision previews, but actual movement checks all collision bodies at execution. AI searches toward last-known positions or sweeps opposing deployment space when it has no known enemy. Full replay and raw export unlock after combat with fog enabled; no fog is available for diagnostics. The existing AI remains heuristic.
- Saved battles embed exact generated battlefield geometry (including settings) and recent team contacts. Existing saves without embedded maps use the original v0.1.6 geometry and no fog, preserving deployment and pending jobs. Map seeds and options are saved in setup. Same-seed rematch retains the exact map. Existing roster data and app ID unchanged. No classes, spells, equipment or advancement introduced.

## v0.1.8 — Tactical AI and coordination

- Basic retains local heuristics; Standard adds team target ranking, threat interception, formation-aware opening movement, bounded tactical retreat, and attribute-based roles; Tactical also compares exact expected damage, action duration, and resource use when choosing attacks. All three use the same legal action and dice engine. No hidden bonuses or additional random draws.
- Configure enemy policy at setup, or both teams during battle. Aggression, caution, coordination and formation influence Standard/Tactical decisions. Orders (advance, focus a visible enemy, protect an ally, regroup) guide automated allies. Formation is an opening preference, not a rigid constraint. Target orders are preferences, not commands to attack impossible targets.
- Individual allied automation is independent of the auto-play both override. Manual control pauses at the next decision of a nonautomated ally. The current action remains committed. Latest AI decision per unit is persisted and inspectable; enemy decisions are hidden during fog-enabled battles. Retreat is limited by a simulated-time cooldown and requires a healthier ally and a position farther from visible threats.
- Save migration supplies defaults for older battles. Exact-seed rematches preserve current AI policy and individual automation, reset transient orders to Advance, and reset the simulation state. Enemy searches use recorded contacts or deployment sweeps rather than hidden locations.
